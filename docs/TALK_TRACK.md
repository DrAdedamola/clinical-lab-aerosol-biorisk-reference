# Talk track

How to explain this out loud. Read it on a timer; change any sentence you would not say.

## The sixty-second version

"Every clinical laboratory in the country is required to assess the biosafety risk of its
own procedures, but nobody gives them a method for doing it one bench step at a time. So I
built one. It is an open scoring instrument, twelve anchored items, mapped to the clause
structure of the international biorisk standard, and I applied it to seven routine bench
steps: specimen reception, centrifugation, uncapping, automated analysers, cartridge-based
molecular testing, mass spectrometry identification, and open culture work.

What came out surprised me. The steps laboratories worry about most, uncapping tubes and
spinning blood, have actually been measured, and the measurements found essentially
nothing. The steps that have infected and killed laboratory workers, almost all of them
open-bench culture work, have never been measured at all. And the highest-throughput step
in the entire laboratory, running specimens on automated analysers, has no evidence of any
kind behind its safety practice.

So bench biosafety is inverted with respect to its evidence base. The instrument makes that
visible because it reports the score and the strength of the evidence side by side and
never combines them. It is free, it is cited to sources anyone can open, and it gives a
laboratory somewhere to start other than a blank form."

## The two-sentence version

"I built a free, open scoring tool that lets a clinical laboratory assess the aerosol
biosafety risk of individual bench steps, with every recommendation cited and graded for
how good the evidence actually is. Applying it showed that the steps that have killed
laboratory workers are the ones nobody has ever measured."

## Three likely questions

**"Isn't this just a risk matrix with extra steps?"**
No, and the difference is the evidence grade. A risk matrix gives you a number and tells
you nothing about whether the number is based on measurement or on tradition. This reports
both and refuses to merge them, so a laboratory can see which of its controls rest on
evidence and which rest on precaution. That distinction is the whole contribution.

**"How do you know your scores are right?"**
I do not, yet, and the project says so. The scores are structured expert judgement with
every anchor written down so anyone can argue with a specific item rather than with the
total. The test of an instrument is whether two people applying it independently agree.
That study is designed, the protocol is published in advance, the analysis code is written,
and it has not been run because I will not publish an agreement figure without a real
second rater. It is the next release.

**"What qualifies you to build this?"**
I was trained in biorisk assessment, characterization and evaluation through the Sandia
National Laboratories biorisk management workshops run for the Defense Threat Reduction
Agency, and I have taught that method to others. What I have done here is take a method
that lives in a workshop room and turn it into a free instrument a laboratory anywhere can
download and use.

## What not to say

- Do not say the instrument is validated. It is not, until the agreement study runs.
- Do not say a laboratory using it is ISO 35001 conformant. The mapping is structural.
- Do not say uncapping is safe. Say one study measured it and recovered nothing.
