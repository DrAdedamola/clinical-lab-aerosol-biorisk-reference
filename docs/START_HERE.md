# Start here

Plain-language guide to this project. Read this first. Everything else can wait.

---

## 1. What you are building, in one sentence

A free, public reference that takes seven everyday bench steps in a hospital
laboratory, says how much is actually known about each one releasing germs into
the air, and gives each step a score and a recommended set of safety controls.

## 2. Why it matters

Every clinical laboratory in the United States is told to assess the risk of its
own procedures. Nobody is told how. A laboratory supervisor who wants to know
whether uncapping blood tubes on the open bench is safe has to start from a blank
form, guess, and hope.

The strange part is that the evidence is thin and scattered. Some of these steps
have been measured directly with air samplers. Some have only killed people, in
reported cases, without ever being measured. Some have never been studied at all
and are governed entirely by tradition. Nobody has laid that out side by side.

This project does. One everyday comparison: it is a nutrition label for bench
safety. The number tells you the risk. The grade beside it tells you how much
to trust the number.

## 3. Where the information comes from

Everything is free and public. No paywalls, no private data, no patients.

- **Published studies** from PubMed Central and open journals that measured
  aerosols during laboratory work, or that reported infections caught at the bench.
- **CDC and WHO guidance** that is free to download, including the BMBL
  (the US biosafety manual) and the WHO Laboratory Biosafety Manual.
- **ASM Sentinel Level Clinical Laboratory Protocols**, free PDFs on asm.org,
  which tell frontline laboratories how to rule out dangerous organisms and
  refer them onward.
- **CMS laboratory statistics**, the government count of how many laboratories exist.
- **ISO 35001:2019**, the international biorisk management standard. We use only
  its clause numbers and titles, which are facts about the standard's structure.
  We do not copy its text. You do not need to buy it to use this reference.

One row of the main output looks like: one bench step, its score, its evidence
grade, the sources behind it, and the controls recommended for it.

## 4. What you will have at the end

1. A **public project page** on GitHub, open to anyone.
2. A **permanent citation number (DOI)** from Zenodo, so other people can cite
   this work the way they cite a journal article.
3. A **scoring instrument** (version 1.0) that any laboratory can download and
   apply to its own bench.
4. **Reference scores and control sets** for the seven bench steps, every claim
   footnoted to a source anyone can open.
5. An **agreement study**: you and a second rater score the same published
   incidents independently, and we report how often you agreed. This is what
   turns an opinion into an instrument.
6. A **sentinel laboratory module** applying the same scoring to rule-out and
   referral steps.
7. A **short technical report** you can cite, attach to an application, or hand
   to a laboratory director.

## 5. How it connects to your work

You were trained in biorisk assessment, characterization, and evaluation through
the Sandia National Laboratories biorisk management workshops run for the US
Defense Threat Reduction Agency, and you have taught that method to others.
This project is that training turned into a public instrument that a laboratory
in Idaho or Lagos can download for free and apply on Monday morning. It is the
difference between having a skill and leaving a tool behind.

## 6. What you need to do, and how long it takes

| You do | Time |
|---|---|
| Answer five questions (they have recommended answers, you can just say "go with your suggestion") | 10 minutes |
| Rule on the provisional scores I draft for each step | 2 to 3 hours |
| Check a sample of citations against their sources | 1 to 2 hours |
| Recruit one second rater and score the scenarios independently | 3 to 4 hours, spread out |
| Approve publishing and create two access tokens | 15 minutes |
| Submit the preprint yourself | 20 minutes |

Everything else is mine.

## 7. The steps ahead

1. **Checkpoint 1** — Project explained, questions asked. (You are here.)
2. **Checkpoint 2** — Evidence collected and logged with its sources.
3. **Checkpoint 3** — Scoring instrument v1.0 drafted and mapped to ISO 35001.
4. **Checkpoint 4** — Provisional scores for the seven steps, for your ruling.
5. **Checkpoint 5** — Agreement harness and scenario set ready for two raters.
6. **Checkpoint 6** — Full package drafted, quality checks run, ready for you to verify.
7. **Verification gate** — You check it. Nothing publishes until you do.
8. **Publishing** — GitHub, then Zenodo DOI, then the preprint you submit yourself.
